FundFlow: Redefining Loan Access
For Small Business Owners

Project Overview

Small businesses often face barriers when securing loans, causing cash flow challenges that limit growth and sustainability. Fund Flow is a digital platform
designed to simplify access to loans, streamline repayment management, and provide personalized financial guidance for small business owners.

Context

4-person UX team, Arizona State University

My Role

UX Designer: User interviews, personas, journey maps, hi-fi mockups, prototype, usability testing, and iterations

Timeline

3 weeks | Nov – Dec 2024

Tools

Figma, FigJam, Canva,
Adobe Creative Suite, Excalidraw,
Google Forms

Why This Project Matters

  • The Challenge: Loan applications are complex, tracking repayments is confusing, and existing calculators are often inaccurate. Marginalized owners face additional hurdles that increase cost and risk.
  • Design Objective: Design an intuitive platform that simplifies loan access, improves tracking, and empowers small business owners to make informed financial decisions.

Figure 1. Fund Flow Desktop Demo: Showcases key features and interactions in a brief, interactive walkthrough.

Exploration & Discovery

My team analyzed competitors, like Lendio, Fundera, and QuickBooks Capital to identify common features, usability patterns, and gaps in existing loan tools.

Figure 2. Competitive Analysis: Highlights industry patterns, gaps, and opportunities for Fund Flow.

I conducted five interviews with small business owners to uncover pain points in loan applications and tracking. These were my key findings:

  • Complex & Frustrating Applications: Users wanted simple, clear steps.
  • Lost in Loan Tracking: Needed dashboards with reminders.
  • Inaccurate Calculators: Desired reliable, tailored financial tools.
  • Forecasting Needs: Wanted tools for better financial planning.

I then synthesized the insights into three personas—Seasonal Sam, Ambitious Ava, and Frugal Fiona.

Figure 3. User Personas: Represents distinct small business owner needs and behaviors.

UX Design Process

User Flow

I created a user flow to outline the path from account login/creation to key loan tools to ensure smooth navigation and logical task completion.

Figure 4. User Flow: Outlines steps from account creation to core loan tools for smooth navigation.

Low-Fidelity Wireframes

The team focused on establishing layout, content hierarchy, and basic interaction patterns for both desktop and mobile screens.

Figure 5. Low-Fidelity Wireframes: Shows early layouts and page structures.

Mid-Fidelity Wireframes

These were refined by adding real copy, consistent iconography, and clearer navigation flows to bring more realism and prepare for the transition into high-fidelity design.

Figure 6. Mid-Fidelity Wireframes: Shows improved navigation, copy, and icons.

Style Guide & Branding

My team finalized a cohesive style guide, including logo, color palette, and typography, establishing a trustworthy and approachable visual identity for Fund Flow.

Figure 7. Style Guide: Shows logo, color palette, and typography, establishing Fund Flow’s visual identity.

High-Fidelity Mockups

From these wireframes and style guide, I refined the visual elements, typography, and interactions to create realistic representations of the platform. I then transformed them into a clickable prototype that let users go through the main loan application and tracking steps.

Figure 8. High-Fidelity Mockups: Showcases polished key screens that reflect the final visual style.

Usability Testing & Design Iterations

I tested the prototype with four small business owners. They suggested adding a document upload feature and clearer guidance in the comparison tool. I updated the prototype step by step based on this feedback to make it easier to use.

Figure 9. Interactive Prototype: Demonstrates updated features.

Impact & Key Learnings

  • Simplified application: Making loan applications simpler really reduced frustration—users could complete steps more smoothly. Learning: Clear instructions and a logical flow make a big difference in confidence.
  • Better guidance: Adding document uploads and better guidance in the comparison tool helped users feel more in control.
    Learning: Small improvements can noticeably improve usability.
  • Future-ready: The design now provides a solid foundation for future features, like personalized loan recommendations.
    Learning: Thinking ahead makes it easier to expand the product without breaking the experience.
