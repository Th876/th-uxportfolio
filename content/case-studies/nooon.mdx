## The problem

nooon connects hotels with influencers to drive direct bookings, but the site couldn't explain that in under five seconds. It wasn't only a visual problem. The direction kept moving: leadership first wanted a social, community-style presence, then came back from an investor event, after seeing how competitors positioned themselves, wanting a serious B2B platform instead. I was the only designer, working with the CEO, developers, and the sales and marketing teams in one-week Agile sprints. I redesigned the site end to end around the new direction and built the English version live in WordPress.

## Key design decisions

<Decision title="Put pricing where people look for it" image="/images/work/nooon/pricing1.webp" alt="nooon pricing navigation, plan cards, and comparison table" caption="The redesigned pricing page">

Pricing was buried two clicks deep in the old site. I moved it into primary navigation and gave it its own page with a clear chart, so hoteliers could self-qualify before ever talking to sales.

</Decision>

<Decision title="Give every page one clear next step" image="/images/work/nooon/home-cta1.webp" alt="Homepage section with a booking form and submit button" caption="Homepage CTA pattern">

Testing showed people liked the site but didn't act on it. I added a consistent "Book&nbsp;a&nbsp;Call" pattern across key pages and tightened the CTA copy, so the path from interest to demo request was never more than one decision away.

</Decision>

<Decision title="Design for where nooon was headed, not just where it was" image="/images/work/nooon/style-guide.png" alt="nooon style guide with color, typography, and icons" caption="Style guide: logo, color, type, icons">

nooon had no real visual identity, just a logo and inconsistent fonts, and had just shifted from wanting a social-style presence to wanting a serious B2B one. I built a full style guide (logo, color, type, icon set) grounded in that new direction, and designed the case-studies section to scale past its current two entries, so the site could grow without another redesign or another pivot.

</Decision>

## Reflection

nooon was the first project where I was the only designer on the team. I was responsible for the design work and reviewed every decision with a CEO, developers, and the sales and marketing teams.

Then the direction changed. After the investor event, leadership moved from a social, community feel to a serious B2B platform. I had already designed a full community homepage, and I treated it as a first direction instead of protecting it. I took what I had learned, rebuilt around the new positioning, and kept the one-week sprints moving.

That pace taught me to make smaller decisions faster and check them with quick internal tests. If I did it again, I'd push for real hoteliers to see the designs earlier, since outside feedback would have sharpened the positioning even more.
