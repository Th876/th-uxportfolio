## The problem

Millennials and Gen Z consistently struggle with existing finance apps that are either too complex or too clinical, especially around debt and emergency savings. For my capstone at Arizona State University, I ran a 30-response survey, 8 interviews, and a competitive analysis, and the research pointed to a gap deeper than another tracker. People didn't know what to do with the numbers once they saw them, they needed something that could guide them without making them feel judged for where they were starting from.

## Key design decisions

<Decision title="Build guidance into onboarding instead of assuming financial literacy" image="/images/work/xpensepal/xp-onboarding.png" alt="XpensePal guided onboarding flow" caption="Guided onboarding flow">

An open card sort with 5 participants showed people needed to understand the app before they'd trust it with their finances. In usability testing, 4 of 5 participants completed key tasks independently after I added this step.

</Decision>

<Decision title="Motivate with rewards and shared milestones, not just data" image="/images/work/xpensepal/xp-rewards.png" alt="XpensePal rewards and milestones screen" caption="Rewards and milestones screen">

Testing showed users disengaged from finance apps that only displayed numbers back at them. I added a rewards system for consistent tracking and a way to share milestones with friends or family, giving people a reason to keep coming back beyond just monitoring balances.

</Decision>

<Decision title="Design accessibility in from the start, not as a fix later" image="/images/work/xpensepal/style-guide.png" alt="XpensePal WCAG-compliant style guide" caption="Style guide: colors, type, accessibility">

Rather than treating accessibility as a pass at the end, I built WCAG-compliant colors, readable fonts, and adjustable settings directly into the style guide from day one, avoiding the redesign work that retrofitting accessibility usually requires.

</Decision>

## Reflection

This was my capstone at Arizona State University, the project meant to show everything the degree built toward, so owning research, design, and testing end to end in two weeks forced real prioritization. I couldn't chase every finding, only the ones that would move the needle for actual financial anxiety and habit-building. The hardest balance was giving Gen Y/Z users enough guidance to feel supported without the app feeling patronizing. The gap I'd still want to close is a longer testing window specifically for the rewards system, two weeks is enough to see if people like a feature, not enough to see if it actually builds a lasting habit.
