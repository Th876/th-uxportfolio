nooon, Redesigning to Connect
Hotels and Influencers

Project Overview

nooon is a Helsinki-based startup that helps hotels collaborate with influencers to drive bookings. Their previous website lacked
a cohesive visual identity, had unclear messaging, and made it hard for hotel and influencer users to understand the platform’s value.
I led a full brand and website redesign to improve clarity, usability, and engagement, and implemented the English version of the site in WordPress,
while collaborating with the CEO, sales, and development teams.

Context

Team Project | Sole UX Designer

My Role

UX/UI Design, Branding, Website Implementation

Timeline

3 weeks | May – July 2025

Tools

Figma, WordPress (Elementor
and Blocksy plugins), HTML/CSS

Why This Redesign Matters

  • The Challenge:The website was visually inconsistent, cluttered, and hard to navigate, leaving visitors confused about Nooon’s value and services.
  • Design Objective: Redesign the website to reflect Nooon’s professional B2B brand, improve clarity and usability, increase engagement, and support conversions.

Figure 1. Before-and-After Hero Section: Shows nooon’s old homepage updated to a clear, cohesive, and user-friendly design.

Exploration & Discovery

I explored B2B SaaS and hospitality websites, including Notion, Monday.com, Mural, and Databox to gather ideas for layouts, typography, and features that reflected the CEO’s vision.

Figure 2. Website Inspirations: Showcases the visual styles and ideas the CEO wanted for nooon’s redesign.

I conducted a competitor analysis to identify missing features that could differentiate XpensePal. Additionally, I conducted a survey with 30 responses and 8 in-depth user interviews to understand user pain points and goals.

Findings showed Millennials and Gen Z struggle with financial literacy, debt, and emergencies, highlighting the need for simplified, guided financial features. I synthesized these insights with an empathy map to define three user personas.

Figure 5. Empathy Map: Highlights user needs for automated tracking, interactive guidance, and a simple
interface to balance daily budgeting with long-term financial goals.

Figure 6. User Personas: Highlight traits and goals guiding the redesigned sign-in flow.

UX Design Process

Early Design Exploration

I ran an open card sort with five participants to understand users’ mental models and shape the app’s information architecture.

Figure 7. Card Sorting Results: Reveals natural feature groupings that guide the information architecture.

User Flow

Based on card sorting insights, I mapped a user flow from account creation to key features to make navigation smooth.

Figure 8. User Flow: Maps the path from account creation to key features.

Wireframes

Low-Fidelity

I started with rough wireframes to quickly test layouts, flows, and guidance cues and, ultimately settled on the versions shown below.

Figure 9. Lo-Fidelity Wireframes: Validate early structure to test user paths and guidance cues.

Mid-Fidelity

Next, I polished the designs by refining copy, interactions, and prompts so navigation felt smoother and more intuitive.

Figure 10. Mid-Fidelity Wireframes: Refine interactions, copy, and prompts to improve usability.

Style Guide

I created a style guide for XpensePal to ensure a consistent and approachable visual identity across the app.

Figure 11. Style Guide: Shows logo, color palette, and typography establishing the app’s visual identity.

Final Design

I translated mid-fidelity wireframes into high-fidelity mockups, and created a fully interactive prototype to simulate key tasks, including setting savings goals, updating budgets, and tracking debt.

Figure 12. High-Fidelity Screens: Showcases key screens like dashboard, budgeting, and savings.

Usability Testing

I then conducted usability tests with 5 participants to observe task completion, identify pain points, and gather actionable feedback for design improvements.

Design Iterations

Based on these insights, I implemented the following in the final design:

  • Rewards System: Allows users to share progress with friends/family, increasing accountability and engagement.
  • Share Milestones: for first-time users
  • Positive Alerts: Provides encouraging messages to reinforce good habits and reduce anxiety around finances.

Figure 13. Interactive Prototype: Demonstrates updated flows and milestone features.

Impact & Key Learnings

  • Guidance matters: Interactive tutorials with step-by-step support drove usability and boosted users’ confidence as 4/5 participants completed tasks independently.
  • User motivation is key: Milestone rewards and sharing features increased engagement; celebrating small wins reinforces habit formation.
  • Accessibility counts: WCAG-compliant colors, readable fonts, and adjustable settings improved usability, proving early accessibility prevents later redesigns.
